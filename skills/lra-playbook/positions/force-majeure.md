## Position
A closed list of events beyond reasonable control; excludes the supplier's
subcontractors' failures, labour disputes within the supplier's own workforce,
and anything the business continuity plan should have covered. Customer may
terminate if the event continues for 30 days, with a pro-rata refund.

## Fallback
An open definition ("including") is acceptable if the exclusions above stay.
Termination after 60 days.

## Walk-away
Force majeure covering "any failure of a supplier or subcontractor". Payment
obligations continuing in full while the services are not delivered. No
termination right for a prolonged event.

## Watch for
- Pandemic or epidemic included after a supplier has already priced the risk.
- The supplier relieved of its business continuity and disaster recovery
  obligations by the same clause.
- Notice requirements on the affected party that never bite.

## Model wording (fallback)
Neither party shall be liable for any failure or delay in performing its
obligations to the extent caused by an event beyond its reasonable control,
excluding any failure of the Supplier's subcontractors, any industrial action
by the Supplier's own personnel, and any event which the Business Continuity
Plan was required to address. If such an event prevents performance for more
than 60 days, the Customer may terminate this Agreement on written notice and
the Supplier shall refund any Charges paid for Services not delivered.
